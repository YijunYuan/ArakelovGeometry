## Construction
Let I be any uncountable set, choose algebraically independent variables y_i (i in I) over Q, and let
L=Q(y_i : i in I), x_i=y_i^2−1, K=Q(x_i : i in I).
The x_i are algebraically independent: substitution X_i -> Y_i^2−1 is injective on every finite polynomial ring (substitution X_i -> Y_i−1 is an automorphism, followed by independent even powers). Each y_i is quadratic algebraic over K, so L/K is algebraic.

On K define v(P/Q)=ord_0(P)−ord_0(Q), where ord_0(P) is the least total degree of a nonzero monomial in the finitely many variables x_i occurring in P. The initial homogeneous forms multiply without vanishing, so ord_0(PQ)=ord_0(P)+ord_0(Q); sums have order at least the minimum. Therefore v is a well-defined Z-valued nonarchimedean valuation. Set |a|=2^(−v(a)) for a !=0 and |0|=0. It is trivial on Q and |x_i|=1/2. The residue field need NOT be Q (ratios x_i/x_j have value 0); nothing in the proof assumes that it is.

Let Ω={ω}, A=P(Ω), ν({ω})=1, and φ(ω)=|·|. Every log evaluation is a finite constant, hence integrable and measurable. This is an adelic curve. It is not proper (log|x_i|=−log2), but Prop3.4.6 does not require properness.

## Independent quadratic directions
For finite J⊂I put E_J=K(y_j:j∈J). Quadratic adjunction gives [E_J:K]≤2^|J|. For each independent choice of signs on J, the automorphism of the rational field L taking y_j to ±y_j and fixing the remaining y_i fixes every x_i, hence K. Its restriction to E_J is distinct. Thus there are ≥2^|J| distinct K-automorphisms, so equality holds. Equivalently the squareclasses of (1+x_i) are independent: a product over a nonempty finite set has odd multiplicity at every distinct prime factor 1+x_i and cannot be a square in the rational field. Thus no hidden collapse of independent coordinates occurs.

## Explicit extensions and finite-fibre exhaustion
For ε∈{±1}^I, define w_ε on L to be the total-degree valuation at the point y_i=ε_i: write z_i=y_i−ε_i and take the least total degree in the z_i, then extend by differences to fractions. Under x_i=2ε_i z_i+z_i^2, the initial homogeneous form of a nonzero polynomial P(x) of order d becomes P_d(2ε_i z_i), which is nonzero of the same degree d. Hence w_ε restricts to v on K. The absolute value 2^(−w_ε) is an actual extension of |·|.

If ε_i=+1 then |y_i−1|_ε=1/2; if ε_i=−1 then |y_i−1|_ε=1, so distinct signs yield distinct extension absolute values.

For self-contained exhaustion, let |·|_w be any extension on E_J. Its restriction to Q is trivial, so it is nonarchimedean: the binomial expansion gives |a+b|^n≤(n+1)max(|a|,|b|)^n, and n-th roots tend to the ultrametric inequality. Since |y_j|^2=|1+x_j|=1, both |y_j±1|≤1. Their product is |x_j|=1/2 and their difference is 2 with norm1. Exactly one sign ε_j satisfies |y_j−ε_j|=1/2, and the other difference has norm1.

Set p_n(T)=sum_{k=0}^n binom(1/2,k) T^k in Q[T]. The identity p_n(T)^2−(1+T) is divisible by T^(n+1), all nonzero rational coefficients have norm1, and |p_n(x_j)−1|≤1/2. Therefore |y_j+ε_j p_n(x_j)|=1, and
|y_j−ε_j p_n(x_j)|≤2^(−n−1).
Thus each y_j is approximated, with a fixed sign-dependent sequence of elements of K, for every extension carrying that sign. A rational expression in the y_j is likewise approximated by the same K-sequence; its denominator is eventually nonzero because it converges to a nonzero element. Its absolute value is the limit of the base absolute values of this sequence. Consequently two extensions with the same signs agree on all of E_J. There are exactly 2^|J| extensions and they are indexed by the signs in J. (Equivalently the binomial series shows all these quadratic directions split in the completion K_v.)

Every finite intermediate field F/K in L is contained in some E_J: take a finite K-basis of F and collect the finitely many y_i used in its elements. Hence the E_J are cofinal among the source's finite stages. Restrictions between their fibres are precisely restriction of sign vectors. Every extension on L is therefore one of the explicit w_ε, and
M_{L,ω} ≅ {±1}^I
as an inverse system and as a topological space with the projective/product topology.

## The sigma algebras differ
Under this identification, A_{L,ω} is the sigma algebra generated by finite sign-coordinate cylinders. Every set in it depends on at most countably many coordinates: the class of countably determined sets contains the finite cylinders and is closed under complements and countable unions. Any singleton is closed in the Hausdorff product topology, hence Borel. It cannot be countably determined because changing a coordinate outside a proposed countable determining set changes the point while retaining all those coordinates. Thus A_{L,ω} is strictly smaller than the Borel sigma algebra, in an actual adelic fibre satisfying every source assumption.

## Consequence and minimum repair
This refutes the *additional equality in the proof*, not the probability construction or the proposition's stated conclusion. The compactness proof of countable additivity on clopen cylinders still works, and Carathéodory gives a measure on the expressly defined A_{L,ω}. Delete the Borel equality and say “probability measure on the cylinder-generated sigma algebra.” Alternatively add a countable cofinal finite-stage hypothesis for this equality; for instance a countable algebraic field L has countably many finite intermediate fields. The report must not suggest that all fibres have unequal sigma algebras.
