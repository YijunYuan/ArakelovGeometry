# S1 (suggest stable ID CM34-S1)
Classification: confirmed false assertion in a proof; theorem not refuted. Severity: major. Confidence: high.
Source: Theorem3.4.12, PDF212/196 states arbitrary successive algebraic extensions L2/L1/K; PDF213/197 after first tower-integral display says `[K2:K1] divides [L2:L1]`, with K1=K2∩L1 and prime upper degree. No relative-algebraic-closure hypothesis on K appears in the theorem, local proof, or §3.4 standing assumptions (PDF204/188). The only “separable closure” near §3.4.1 is the construction of a finite extension's separable part; it does not make K relatively algebraically closed in L1 and does not apply as a hidden standing assumption here.
Witness: a=real cube root2, ζ primitive cube root of unity; K=Q, K2=Q(a), L1=Q(ζa), L2=Q(a,ζ). T^3−2 is Eisenstein2, so both cubic fields have degree3. K2⊂R while L1 contains a nonreal element, so they differ. Their intersection has degree dividing3; degree3 would force equality, hence intersection=Q. Since ζ=(ζa)/a, their compositum is L2. Since ζ∉Q(a) and ζ has quadratic minimal polynomial, [L2:Q]=6 and [L2:L1]=2 (prime). K2 is a finite K-subextension of L2, K2⊄L1, all extensions are separable. Thus every genuine hypothesis preceding the asserted divisibility holds, but 3 does not divide2. Any singleton-measure adelic structure on Q (e.g. usual real absolute value) completes the source setting; properness is not needed.
Smallest correction: discard the intersection-degree inference, descend a B-basis of N plus multiplication coefficients, prescribed finite subset and separable-generator data to a sufficiently large finite A/K in B; obtain finite E/A with B(E)=N, [E:A]=[N:B] and matching separable degree. This is the accepted repair.
IMPORTANT DIFFERENCE FROM OLD REPORT: the same cubic example cannot separately refute the later irreducibility sentence *under all of its local assumptions*. Once `[K2:K1]=[L2:L1]=p` and `L2=L1(a)` are assumed, the degree of the minimal polynomial of a over L1 is p, and P (degree p) is indeed minimal. The cubic example has degree3 below and2 above, so it fails that already-incorrect equality. Treat later polynomial use as a consequence of the bad degree setup, not an independent irreducibility defect. The bare phrase “prime degree and a not in L1 imply irreducibility” is false in isolation, but isolation drops load-bearing local context.
Prime reduction: PDF213/197 paragraph beginning “By induction ... prime number” is unproved as written: an arbitrary finite field extension need not admit a tower with prime-degree factors. Do not inflate this to a counterexample to tower compatibility; the finite-descent repair removes this reduction entirely.
