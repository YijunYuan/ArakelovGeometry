## Algebraic tower proof: finite descent

PDF 213 reduces to a prime upper degree and asserts that
`[F : F ∩ B]` divides `[N : B]`. This does not hold for arbitrary
subfields: distinct cubic subfields of the splitting field of `T³ − 2`
over the rationals have trivial intersection and compositum of relative
degree two. The blueprint's `lem:finite-descent-separable` instead descends
a basis, multiplication coefficients, and the separable part to a finite
base. The resulting base-change isomorphisms preserve both the total and
separable degrees. `lem:descended-base-change` then proves the fibre-weight
identity by counting embeddings into algebraically closed completed fields.
The tower theorem's conclusion is retained.

## Cylinder sigma algebra

PDF 209 identifies the sigma algebra generated by finite cylinders with the
full Borel sigma algebra of an arbitrary projective-limit topology. Such an
identification is not generally valid for uncountable systems. The blueprint
uses compactness only to prove countable additivity on the cylinder algebra;
Carathéodory extension then gives the measure on the specified cylinder sigma
algebra. This preserves the measurable space defined on PDF 208 and the
probability construction in Proposition 3.4.6 without requiring that additional
Borel claim.

## Infinite-fibre integrability

Proposition 3.4.9 (PDF 211), read with the preceding definition on PDF 210,
uses the nonnegative integral of the absolute value to test integrability.
The faithful Lean criterion is finiteness of the double ENNReal integral of
`ENNReal.ofReal ‖f‖`. A real Bochner integral is totalized at zero when its
integrand is not integrable, so its value cannot replace this nonnegative
criterion on an infinite fibre. The signed operator is used on almost every
integrable fibre, and its L1 construction respects almost-everywhere equality.

## The archimedean error in Northcott finiteness

Lemma 3.5.5 (PDF 217) uses the mass of the archimedean locus in its
polynomial product bound. Section 3.5 imposes no explicit finite-mass
hypothesis. The adelic axioms give integrability of the archimedean exponent
`κ`, but do not imply that the measure of its positive locus is finite.

The formalization proves the sharper real bound with finite error
`log(min(deg F + 1, deg G + 1)) * ∫ κ`. The exponent is preserved by
restriction of places and its integral by algebraic extension. Since
`0 ≤ κ ≤ 1` on the archimedean locus and it vanishes elsewhere, the printed
mass bound follows in extended nonnegative reals, with `0 * ∞ = 0`; it also
holds as a real inequality whenever that mass is finite. The finite exponent
bound is then used for minimal-polynomial coefficients and Northcott finiteness.
No finite-mass hypothesis is added to Theorem 3.5.3 or Corollary 3.5.7.

Conjugate root heights are compared using two finite embeddings of a simple
extension, instead of passing through its normal closure. Claim 3.5.6 is proved
for every algebraic extension and suffices directly for the corollary.

## Horizontal places in the rational-function morphism

Example 3.7.2(3) on PDF 220 describes the horizontal places as the closed
points of the projective line. The construction it explicitly refers to,
Section 3.2.5 on PDF 188–189, uses the closed points of the affine line
`ℙ¹_ℚ \ {∞}`. The formalization follows that construction: horizontal places
are height-one prime ideals of `ℚ[T]`, with the Mahler weights prescribed
there. This preserves the source curve and the morphism's three components;
it does not add another horizontal point. Both passages have registered
vision transcriptions.

The Section 3.2.5 adelic structure is constructed as a prerequisite to the
morphism. Its separate product-formula assertion is not required by
Definition 3.7.1 and is not claimed as part of this task.
